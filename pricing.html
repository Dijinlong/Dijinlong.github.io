Pricing 103 models across 51 sellers: how the discount list is built

Published 2026-11-21 · LONG AI

"Up to 78% below list price" is a marketing number. It is also, in practice, the least useful number in an aggregator, because the discount differs per model, per seller and per token direction. Here is how we actually build the price table and where the headline discount comes from.

Discount is per model, not per seller

A seller offering 2.2x on GPT-class models may be at 6x on image models. Publishing one number per seller forces you to either advertise the best case or the average, and both mislead. We list the price of each of the 103 models with the seller mix behind it, and the headline figure is simply the best observed ratio at publication time.

Input and output tokens are priced separately

  • Chat models: input and output multipliers differ, sometimes by 4x.
  • Reasoning models: output dominates, so a "cheap" channel with long outputs can lose to a pricier one.
  • Image and video models: priced per generation or per second, not per token — they cannot be merged into the same table without a unit column.

Why the cheapest seller is rarely the default

Below roughly 20% of list price, timeout rates and protocol defects climb fast. We keep those channels available but not primary: the route selection prefers a channel whose error rate stays under control, and only falls back to the cheapest when the primary is saturated. The savings from a failing channel are consumed by retries and by refunds, so effective cost can exceed list.

Settlement currency and rounding

Upstreams quote in USD, users top up in CNY. We settle at a fixed published rate rather than a live one, and round at the token level once per request, not per chunk. Both choices are boring, and both avoid the "small balance drift" complaints that per-chunk rounding produces.
