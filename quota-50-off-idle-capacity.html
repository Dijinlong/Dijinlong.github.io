The question worth answering is not "who is cheap" but "why can anyone sell official-price quota at half price at all". The answer is idle capacity, and it changes how you should buy.

The 50% ceiling is a rule, not a market price

The platform caps a seller quote at half the vendor's official price (5 折) and puts no floor underneath. That ceiling is a policy against list-price undercutting games, not a clearing price discovered by supply and demand. What it produces is predictable: sellers who want volume go as low as they can, sellers who want to keep a listing alive park at the ceiling.

In the 2026-11-25 17:46 snapshot there are 122 live models, 63 vendors, and 122 listings with a computable in-site price — a 100% coverage rate. Average discount is 1.43 折, median 0.5 折. The median being far below the mean is the fingerprint of a distribution with a hard cap and a deep floor.

Where the discount comes from: prepaid quota with a clock

Most sellers are not reselling per-token purchases. They hold prepaid or volume-committed quota bought at or near official price, often with a monthly or contractual expiry. Once that quota is paid for, the marginal cost of serving one more request is close to zero — but only until the clock runs out, after which unused quota is worth nothing.

That asymmetry sets the price. Early in a cycle a seller has little reason to discount deeply: the quota will probably be used. Late in the cycle, unsold quota is a sunk cost, and any revenue above zero is better than expiry. This is why the deepest discounts cluster in the last part of a billing period, and why the same model can be at 1 折 on the 3rd and 0.3 折 on the 28th.

Why the middle of the range is empty

1–4 折 holds only 12 models, 9.9% of the catalogue, while both ends together hold 72.2%. The middle is economically unstable. A seller at 2 折 is not cheap enough to win a price-driven order against a 0.5 折 neighbour, and not expensive enough to earn back anything meaningful if their quota is already prepaid. There is no reason to stand there: if you are clearing idle capacity, go to the floor; if you are protecting margin, sit at the ceiling and wait for a buyer who needs that specific model.

This is also why "average discount" is a poor buying signal. The mean is dragged upward by the 18 models parked at the ceiling; the median tells you what a typical order actually pays. Both numbers are on the model plaza, which lists each model with its current deepest quote.

The stability trade-off: idle capacity is idle until it isn't

Deep discounts have a cost that the price column does not show. A seller clearing month-end quota may stop serving the model entirely next cycle, or serve it more slowly under load. That is not malice; it is the same inventory logic that produced the discount. So the money saved on a 0.5 折 quote can be spent twice if your production traffic depends on it and the listing goes quiet.

Three practical rules follow. First, split traffic: put latency-tolerant batch work on the deepest quotes and keep interactive paths on listings that have been continuously available. Second, re-check the quote before you scale rather than after; a live price table is the only source that reflects a seller changing stance mid-cycle. Third, watch the unit as carefully as the discount: 93 text models here are priced per million input tokens, but 2 text models are priced per second and the 15 video models are priced per second as well — a deep discount on a per-second listing is not comparable to a per-token one without converting first.

How to check a marketplace before you commit

Three checks, all doable from the outside. Is the quote expressed against a stated official price, so you can compute the ratio yourself? Is the deepest quote per model shown, or only a headline "up to" number? And is there a dated snapshot you can point at later, so that a dispute about "it was cheaper last week" has an answer? The integration docs cover how billing and quota are reported per request, which matters more than the discount once you are actually running traffic.

Idle capacity is a real source of cheap tokens, and the 50% ceiling is a real constraint that keeps the market from turning into a race to zero. Buying well means treating a deep quote as inventory with an expiry, not as a permanent price.
